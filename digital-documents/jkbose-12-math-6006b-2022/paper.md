### Q1 — **(C) Transitive**
On $A = \{1,2,3\}$, $R = \{(x,y) : x > y\} = \{(2,1), (3,1), (3,2)\}$.
- **Not reflexive:** $(1,1) \notin R$ since $1 > 1$ is false.
- **Not symmetric:** $(2,1) \in R$ but $(1,2) \notin R$.
- **Transitive:** whenever $x > y$ and $y > z$, we have $x > z$; here $(3,2), (2,1) \in R$ and indeed $(3,1) \in R$. ✔

### Q2 — **(C) $\frac{2\pi}{3}$**
The principal range of $\cos^{-1}$ is $[0, \pi]$. We need $\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{2}$ with $\theta \in [0,\pi]$: $\theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$.

### Q3 — **(B) Skew symmetric matrix**
$(AB - BA)^T = (AB)^T - (BA)^T = B^T A^T - A^T B^T = BA - AB = -(AB - BA)$, using $A^T = A$, $B^T = B$. A matrix equal to the negative of its transpose is skew-symmetric.

### Q4 — **(C) $(0, 0, 1)$**
The unit vector along the $z$-axis is $\hat{k} = 0\hat{i} + 0\hat{j} + 1\hat{k}$; its direction cosines are its components: $(0, 0, 1)$.

### Q5 — $x = 3$, $y = 3$
LHS $= \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 0 & 2x \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} y & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2+y & 6 \\ 1 & 2x+2 \end{bmatrix}$.
Equating with $\begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 1 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$: $2 + y = 5 \Rightarrow y = 3$, and $2x + 2 = 8 \Rightarrow x = 3$.

### Q6 — $\frac{4}{3}e^{3x} + x + C$
$\int (4e^{3x} + 1)\,dx = 4 \cdot \frac{e^{3x}}{3} + x + C = \frac{4}{3}e^{3x} + x + C$.

### Q7 — Verified
$y = x^2 + 2x + C \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x + 2$. Substituting: $\frac{dy}{dx} - 2x - 2 = (2x + 2) - 2x - 2 = 0$. ✔ Hence $y$ is a solution.

### Q8 — $8\pi$ cm²/cm
$A = \pi r^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r$. At $r = 4$: $\frac{dA}{dr} = 8\pi$ cm²/cm.

### Q9 — $\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{7}\right)$
$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = (1)(3) + (-2)(-2) + (3)(1) = 3 + 4 + 3 = 10$.
$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 9} = \sqrt{14}$, $|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{14}$.
$\cos\theta = \frac{10}{\sqrt{14}\cdot\sqrt{14}} = \frac{10}{14} = \frac{5}{7} \Rightarrow \theta = \cos^{-1}\frac{5}{7}$.

### Q10 — $Z_{\max} = 16$ at $(0, 4)$
Feasible region: triangle with corners $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$, $(0,4)$ (bounded by $x + y \le 4$ in the first quadrant).
$Z(0,0) = 0$, $Z(4,0) = 12$, $Z(0,4) = 16$. Maximum $Z = 16$ at $(0, 4)$.

### Q11 — $P(A|B) = \frac{4}{5}$
$P(A \cap B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cup B) = \frac{6}{11} + \frac{5}{11} - \frac{7}{11} = \frac{4}{11}$.
$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = \frac{4/11}{5/11} = \frac{4}{5}$.

### Q12 — $P(A \text{ or } B) = 0.72$
Independence $\Rightarrow P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B) = 0.3 \times 0.6 = 0.18$.
$P(A \cup B) = 0.3 + 0.6 - 0.18 = 0.72$.

### Q13 — $f^{-1}(y) = \frac{y - 3}{4}$
- **One-one:** $f(x_1) = f(x_2) \Rightarrow 4x_1 + 3 = 4x_2 + 3 \Rightarrow x_1 = x_2$.
- **Onto:** for any $y \in \mathbb{R}$, $x = \frac{y-3}{4} \in \mathbb{R}$ satisfies $f(x) = y$.
Being one-one and onto, $f$ is invertible, with $f^{-1}(y) = \frac{y - 3}{4}$.

### Q14 — $\frac{\pi}{4} - x$
Divide numerator and denominator by $\cos x$:
$$\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{1 - \tan x}{1 + \tan x}\right] = \tan^{-1}\left[\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{4} - x\right)\right] = \frac{\pi}{4} - x.$$

### Q15 — Symmetric + skew-symmetric decomposition
$A^T = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ -2 & 3 & -2 \\ -4 & 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Symmetric part $P = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^T) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -\frac{3}{2} & -\frac{3}{2} \\ -\frac{3}{2} & 3 & 1 \\ -\frac{3}{2} & 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$; skew part $Q = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^T) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{5}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 3 \\ \frac{5}{2} & -3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Check: $P^T = P$, $Q^T = -Q$, and $P + Q = A$. ✔

### Q16 — $3a - 3b = 2$, i.e. $a = b + \frac{2}{3}$
Continuity at $x = 3$ requires $\lim_{x \to 3^-} f = \lim_{x \to 3^+} f = f(3)$:
$3a + 1 = 3b + 3 \Rightarrow 3a - 3b = 2$.

### Q17 — Increasing on $(-\infty, -2) \cup (3, \infty)$; decreasing on $(-2, 3)$
$f'(x) = 12x^2 - 12x - 72 = 12(x^2 - x - 6) = 12(x - 3)(x + 2)$.
$f' > 0$ for $x < -2$ or $x > 3$ (strictly increasing); $f' < 0$ for $-2 < x < 3$ (strictly decreasing).

### Q18 — Tangent: $10x + y = 5$; Normal: $x - 10y + 50 = 0$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = 4x^3 - 18x^2 + 26x - 10$; at $(0, 5)$ the slope is $-10$.
Tangent: $y - 5 = -10(x - 0) \Rightarrow 10x + y = 5$.
Normal slope $= \frac{1}{10}$: $y - 5 = \frac{1}{10}x \Rightarrow x - 10y + 50 = 0$.

### Q19 — $\tan^{-1} y = \tan^{-1} x + C$
Separate variables: $\frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = \frac{dx}{1 + x^2}$. Integrating both sides gives $\tan^{-1} y = \tan^{-1} x + C$.

### Q20 — $12\pi$ square units
Here $a = 4$, $b = 3$. By symmetry, area $= 4\int_0^4 \frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16 - x^2}\,dx = 3\left[\frac{x\sqrt{16-x^2}}{2} + 8\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{4}\right]_0^4 = 3(8 \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}) = 12\pi$.
(Equivalently, area of ellipse $= \pi a b = \pi \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 12\pi$.)

$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & -7 & 7 \\ 3 & -2 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(-14 + 14) - \hat{j}(2 - 21) + \hat{k}(-2 + 21) = 19\hat{j} + 19\hat{k}.$$

### Q23 — $Z_{\min} = 60$ at $(5, 5)$; $Z_{\max} = 180$ on segment $(15,15)$–$(0,20)$

### Q24 — Determinant proof / $A^{-1}$
**Main:** Apply $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:
$$\begin{vmatrix} a & a+b & a+b+c \\ 0 & a & 2a+b \\ 0 & 3a & 7a+3b \end{vmatrix}$$
Expanding along the first column: $a\left[a(7a+3b) - 3a(2a+b)\right] = a(7a^2 + 3ab - 6a^2 - 3ab) = a \cdot a^2 = a^3$. ∎

**Or:** $|A| = 1(16-9) - 3(4-3) + 3(3-4) = 7 - 3 - 3 = 1$.
Cofactor matrix $= \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -1 & -1 \\ -3 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, so $\text{adj } A = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -3 & -3 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
Direct multiplication gives $A(\text{adj }A) = I = |A| I$ ✔, and $A^{-1} = \frac{\text{adj }A}{|A|} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -3 & -3 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

### Q25 — Implicit differentiation / second-order ODE
**Main:** Let $u = x^y$, $v = y^x$, so $u + v = 1$.
$\frac{du}{dx} = x^y\left(\frac{y}{x} + \log x \frac{dy}{dx}\right)$, $\frac{dv}{dx} = y^x\left(\log y + \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}\right)$.
Setting $\frac{du}{dx} + \frac{dv}{dx} = 0$ and solving:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{y\,x^{y-1} + y^x \log y}{x^y \log x + x\,y^{x-1}}.$$

**Or:** $y_1 = \frac{-3\sin(\log x) + 4\cos(\log x)}{x} \Rightarrow x y_1 = -3\sin(\log x) + 4\cos(\log x)$.
Differentiating: $y_1 + x y_2 = \frac{-3\cos(\log x) - 4\sin(\log x)}{x} = -\frac{y}{x}$.
Multiplying by $x$: $x y_1 + x^2 y_2 = -y \Rightarrow x^2 y_2 + x y_1 + y = 0$. ∎

### Q27 — Linear ODE / homogeneous ODE
**Main:** Standard form: $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{2}{x}y = x\log x$. Integrating factor $= e^{\int \frac{2}{x}dx} = x^2$.
$\frac{d}{dx}(y x^2) = x^3 \log x \Rightarrow y x^2 = \frac{x^4}{4}\log x - \frac{x^4}{16} + C$.
$$y = \frac{x^2}{4}\log x - \frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{C}{x^2}.$$

**Or:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{2xy} = F\!\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$ — degree-0 homogeneous. Put $y = vx$:
$v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{v^2 - 1}{2v} \Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx} = -\frac{1 + v^2}{2v} \Rightarrow \int\frac{2v\,dv}{1+v^2} = -\int\frac{dx}{x}$.
$\log(1 + v^2) = -\log x + \log C \Rightarrow x(1 + v^2) = C \Rightarrow \boxed{x^2 + y^2 = Cx}$.

### Q28 — Plane through intersection / line–plane angle
